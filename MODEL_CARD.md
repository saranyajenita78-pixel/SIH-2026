# MODEL_CARD.md

## Model 1: Equipment Anomaly Detection (Predictive Maintenance)

- **Purpose:** Detect equipment units (generators, heating plants, communication arrays)
  drifting from their own recent normal operating behaviour, to support predictive
  maintenance prioritization.
- **Intended use:** Decision support for the technical/maintenance team — "which unit should
  I look at first" — not an automated shutdown or control system.
- **Non-intended use:** Must not be used as the sole basis for a real maintenance or safety
  decision on real equipment; must not be presented as validated against real failure data.
- **Data:** Simulated hourly telemetry (`temperature_c`, `vibration_mm_s`, `load_pct`,
  `voltage_v`) per equipment unit, generated by `src/data/simulator.py`. Labeled
  `SIMULATED_DEMO`.
- **Features:** The four raw telemetry channels above.
- **Method:**
  1. Per-unit baseline (mean, std) learned from the earlier 60% of that unit's own history.
  2. Primary score: smoothed (6-hour trailing window) multivariate z-score (RMS across
     features) against the baseline, mapped to a 0–1 probability via a smooth exponential
     curve.
  3. Secondary confirmation: an IsolationForest (80 trees, contamination ≈ 8%) fit on the
     same baseline, reported as an independent flag.
- **Why not deep learning:** Per-unit history is small (hundreds of points); a transparent
  statistical method is both explainable and sufficient, per the project's methodology
  ("do not use deep learning unless necessary").
- **Evaluation:** No real labeled failure data exists for this simulated telemetry. Evaluated
  via internal diagnostic: the model correctly assigns higher anomaly probability to units
  under the `EQUIPMENT_ANOMALY` / `COMPOUND_EMERGENCY` demo scenarios than under `NORMAL`
  (verified in `tests/test_models.py::test_anomaly_scenario_shows_higher_risk_than_normal`).
  Precision/recall are not reported because no ground truth exists — reporting them would be
  fabricated.
- **Output labeling:** All outputs carry `data_status = MODEL_PREDICTION`.

## Model 2: Energy Consumption Forecast

- **Purpose:** Short-horizon (next-hour) forecast of generator load/energy consumption, to
  support fuel and load-management planning.
- **Intended use:** Decision support for energy planning; not a substitute for real-time
  fuel-safety monitoring.
- **Non-intended use:** Must not be used to certify real fuel adequacy for an actual station.
- **Data:** Simulated hourly `consumption_kw` series (driven by weather + scenario), labeled
  `SIMULATED_DEMO`.
- **Features:** Lags at 1, 2, 3, 6, 24 hours; 6-hour rolling mean; hour-of-day.
- **Method:** RandomForestRegressor (100 trees, max depth 8), compared against a naive
  previous-hour persistence baseline.
- **Validation:** Time-aware split (last ~20% of the series held out, never shuffled with
  training data) — prevents future-information leakage, per project methodology.
- **Metrics:** MAE, RMSE, R² for both baseline and model, shown side-by-side on the Energy
  Intelligence and Model Performance pages every run. A separate final model trained on all
  available rows produces the next-hour consumption estimate shown to the operator.
- **Limitations:** Metrics describe fit to the simulated demo series only; no real station
  energy-demand data was available to validate against.
- **Output labeling:** `data_status = MODEL_PREDICTION`.

## Model 3: Operational Risk Engine

- **Purpose:** Convert per-domain signals (weather, equipment, energy, inventory, logistics,
  communication) into a transparent 0–100 risk score per domain and overall.
- **Method:** Rule-based, configurable thresholds and weights (`config.py`) — not a trained
  classifier. This is a deliberate choice: because this score directly drives alerts and
  recommendations shown to operators, full explainability was prioritized over any marginal
  accuracy gain from a black-box model.
- **Evaluation:** Correctness is verified by unit tests asserting monotonic, sensible
  behaviour (e.g., extreme weather values yield HIGH/CRITICAL, mild values yield LOW/MEDIUM;
  see `tests/test_risk.py`).
- **Limitations:** Thresholds are illustrative defaults suited to the simulated telemetry
  ranges used in this prototype; they are not calibrated against real historical Antarctic
  operational incident data (none was available for this project).
- **Output labeling:** `data_status = MODEL_PREDICTION`.

## General statement

No model in this project claims a specific accuracy figure that was not actually measured
and reproducible from the code in this repository. Every model output shown in the dashboard
is visibly tagged `MODEL_PREDICTION` and is treated throughout as decision support, never as
a certainty or an official instruction.
